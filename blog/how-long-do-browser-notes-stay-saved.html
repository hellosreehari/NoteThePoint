How Long Do Browser Notes Stay Saved? Storage Limits & Eviction Explained

When you write inside a no-account notepad like NoteThePoint, one question naturally comes to mind: if there's no server account holding my text, how long will my browser notes stay saved?

In standard desktop or mobile browsers, notes stored in IndexedDB have no automatic expiration date. They survive closing tabs, quitting the browser, and rebooting your computer—often staying intact for years. However, exact persistence rules vary slightly across Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge under low-disk or privacy-cleanup scenarios. Here is everything you should know about browser note storage lifespan.

No Fixed Timer vs. Browser Eviction Rules

Unlike HTTP cookies—which often carry explicit Expires or Max-Age headers—IndexedDB records do not come with a built-in countdown clock. Your notes stay saved on disk indefinitely unless one of three things happens:

  1. You manually clear site data: Selecting "Cookies and other site data" (Chrome/Edge) or "Manage Website Data" (Safari/Firefox) deletes the IndexedDB database for that origin. Note that clearing only "Cached images and files" usually leaves IndexedDB untouched—though double-check your browser checkboxes before clicking clear.
  2. Severe disk pressure (Storage Eviction): If your computer's or phone's SSD/storage runs critically low, browsers follow an LRU (Least Recently Used) cleanup policy, purging storage from websites you have not visited in a long time—starting with inactive sites first.
  3. Safari's 7-day script-writable storage cap: Under Apple's Intelligent Tracking Prevention (ITP), Safari on macOS and iOS can purge client-side storage (like IndexedDB and localStorage) for any origin you don't interact with at all for 7 consecutive days—unless the site is installed as a Home Screen web app on iOS/iPadOS.
Safari Tip: If you rely on Safari for long-term browser notes on an iPhone or Mac, visit your saved notepad at least once a week—or add NoteThePoint to your iOS Home Screen—and periodically back up critical notes using Copy to Clipboard or Print to PDF.

How Much Can Browser Notes Storage Hold?

Older browser scratchpads used localStorage, which hits a hard wall at 5 MB across all keys for a website. Modern tools like NoteThePoint use IndexedDB, where browsers allocate storage as a dynamic share of available disk space (often up to 60% of free disk in Chromium-based browsers and up to 50%–80% pools in Firefox/Safari depending on OS availability).

Even if a long rich-text note takes 20 KB, 500 MB of available IndexedDB space could store tens of thousands of detailed notes before coming anywhere near a storage ceiling.

Three Habits to Make Sure You Never Lose Saved Notes

  • Separate "Cached Files" from "Site Data": Whenever troubleshooting a slow website, never choose "All sites" + "Cookies and site data" unless you are prepared to erase local web app data. Instead, clear cache per site or inspect site storage individually.
  • Use built-in Copy & Print to PDF: In NoteThePoint, the top toolbar includes one-click Copy to Clipboard (preserving rich formatting as well as plain text) and Print Note (allowing clean Save-as-PDF archiving in any OS). For complete backup steps, see our browser notes backup guide.
  • Never store permanent notes in Incognito: Private browsing is engineered to leave zero trace once closed. Use it deliberately when writing sensitive temporary text on a shared computer, not for permanent storage.
