5 Best eTextPad Alternatives for Sharing and Saving Text Online

eTextPad has been a popular choice for anyone who needs a quick, free online text editor. It's a cloud-based notepad and pastebin that lets you store text, share it via URLs, create an account for managing notes, and even password-protect sensitive content. It works, and it works simply.

But depending on your workflow, eTextPad might not be the perfect fit. Maybe you need offline access. Maybe you want rich text formatting, end-to-end encryption, or a tool that doesn't require any account at all. The good news: there are excellent eTextPad alternatives in 2026 that cover every one of these gaps.

Here are the five best free online text editors and notepads worth considering as an eTextPad alternative — each with a genuinely different approach to writing, saving, and sharing text.

1. NoteThePoint — The Privacy-First Browser Notepad

NoteThePoint is an entirely different animal from eTextPad. While eTextPad sends your text to a cloud server, NoteThePoint stores everything locally in your browser's IndexedDB — a powerful, transactional database engine built into every modern browser. No data ever leaves your machine. There is no server, no account, no sign-up form.

This makes NoteThePoint the strongest eTextPad alternative for anyone who values privacy above all else. Your notes are physically incapable of being intercepted, leaked, or moderated by a third party because they never travel over a network.

What makes it stand out:

  • Zero accounts, zero cloud: Open the page and start typing. Your notes exist only on your device.
  • Rich text editing: Bold, italic, headings, bullet lists, numbered lists — formatting that eTextPad doesn't offer.
  • Full offline support: Works without any internet connection after the first load. Write on a flight, in a subway, anywhere.
  • Dark mode: A proper dark/light theme toggle that follows your system preference or manual selection.
  • Unlimited notes with sidebar: Organize dozens or hundreds of notes with full-text search across all of them.
  • Zero note-content tracking: Basic anonymized site analytics and ads never read your text—no server or third-party script receives what you type.

Best for: Personal notes, journaling, drafting sensitive content, quick scratchpad use, offline writing, and anyone who refuses to send their text to someone else's server.

2. Pastebin — The Original Code and Text Sharing Platform

Pastebin is the grandfather of online text sharing. If eTextPad is a notepad with sharing features, Pastebin is a sharing platform with a notepad attached. It's been around since 2002 and remains the default choice for developers who need to share code snippets, error logs, or configuration files quickly.

What makes it stand out:

  • Syntax highlighting: Supports over 250 programming languages, making code far more readable than plain text.
  • Expiration controls: Set pastes to expire after 10 minutes, an hour, a day, or never — giving you fine-grained control over how long shared text lives.
  • Public, unlisted, or private pastes: Choose who can see your content.
  • Massive community: The largest pastebin with an established ecosystem of tools, browser extensions, and API integrations.
  • API access: Programmatically create and retrieve pastes, which is useful for automated workflows.

Best for: Developers sharing code snippets, teams exchanging error logs and configurations, and anyone who needs syntax highlighting for technical text.

3. PrivateBin — The Self-Hostable, Zero-Knowledge Paste Service

PrivateBin takes a radically different approach from both eTextPad and Pastebin. It's an open-source, self-hostable paste service where the server has zero knowledge of what you paste. All content is encrypted in your browser before it's sent, and the decryption key is part of the URL fragment — meaning the server never sees it.

What makes it stand out:

  • End-to-end encryption: Content is encrypted client-side using AES-256. The server stores only ciphertext it cannot read.
  • Self-hostable: Run your own instance on any PHP-capable server. Full control over your data infrastructure.
  • Zero-knowledge architecture: Even the server administrator cannot read your pastes.
  • Burn after reading: Option to destroy the paste after it's been viewed once — perfect for sensitive information.
  • Discussion threads: Encrypted comment threads on pastes, useful for secure collaboration.
  • No accounts required: Create encrypted pastes without signing up.

Best for: Security-conscious users, teams sharing sensitive data, organizations that need self-hosted infrastructure, and anyone who wants encrypted sharing without trusting a third party.

4. Rentry.co — The Markdown-Powered Pastebin

Rentry.co occupies a unique niche as a pastebin that renders Markdown into clean, readable pages. If eTextPad gives you plain text with a URL, Rentry gives you a formatted document with a custom URL. Think of it as a micro-publishing platform for people who know Markdown.

What makes it stand out:

  • Full Markdown rendering: Headings, bold, italic, code blocks, tables, images, and links — all rendered beautifully.
  • Custom URLs: Choose your own URL slug (e.g., rentry.co/my-notes) instead of getting a random hash.
  • Editable pastes: Unlike many pastebins, you can go back and edit your content after publishing with an edit code.
  • Clean, minimal design: No ads, no clutter. The focus is entirely on the content.
  • No account required: Create and edit pastes without signing up.

Best for: Writers and developers who think in Markdown, anyone creating documentation or guides to share via link, and users who want formatted pages without spinning up a full website.

5. Simplenote — The Cross-Platform Note Syncing App

Simplenote by Automattic (the company behind WordPress) is the closest to a traditional note-taking app on this list. Unlike eTextPad's browser-only approach, Simplenote has native apps for Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android — all syncing through the cloud.

What makes it stand out:

  • True cross-platform sync: Write on your phone, continue on your laptop, finish on your tablet. Everything syncs instantly.
  • Markdown support: Toggle Markdown preview for formatted text without leaving the app.
  • Version history: Scrub through every version of a note with a timeline slider. Never lose a previous draft.
  • Tags and search: Organize notes with tags and search across your entire library.
  • Collaboration: Share notes with other Simplenote users for real-time co-editing.
  • Completely free: No premium tier, no paywalls. All features are available to every user.

Best for: Users who need their notes available on every device, writers who want Markdown support with version history, and teams that need lightweight real-time collaboration.

Note: Simplenote requires a free account for syncing, which is a trade-off compared to no-account tools like NoteThePoint or PrivateBin.

Why Look for an eTextPad Alternative?

eTextPad is a solid tool, but it has clear limitations that push users toward alternatives:

  • Plain text only: No bold, headings, or formatting. For anything beyond raw text, you need a different tool.
  • Cloud dependency: Every note lives on eTextPad's servers. No internet means no access, and you're trusting a third party with your content.
  • No offline mode: If you need to write without connectivity, eTextPad can't help.
  • Limited encryption: Password protection is available, but it's server-side — not true end-to-end encryption.
  • No WYSIWYG rich text formatting: While eTextPad supports syntax highlighting for code and markup, it doesn't provide visual headings, formatted bullet lists, or live WYSIWYG editing.

None of these are deal-breakers if you just need quick text sharing. But if your needs have grown beyond that, one of the five alternatives above will serve you better.

Final Thoughts

The landscape of free online text editors in 2026 is surprisingly diverse. eTextPad carved out a useful niche as a clean, simple cloud notepad — but it's no longer the only game in town, and depending on your workflow, it may not even be the best one.

If privacy is non-negotiable, NoteThePoint keeps your notes entirely local with zero cloud exposure. If you need encrypted sharing, PrivateBin offers zero-knowledge security. If you're a developer, Pastebin's syntax highlighting is unmatched. If you think in Markdown, Rentry turns your text into polished pages. And if you need your notes on every device you own, Simplenote syncs seamlessly across platforms.

The best eTextPad alternative is the one that matches how you actually work — not the one with the longest feature list.
